## Executive Summary

I have selected **Sectra AB (publ)** (SECT B, Nasdaq Stockholm, Large Cap) — a Swedish medical-imaging IT and cybersecurity company — for the BUS 629 ratios project. Sectra is a non-U.S. IFRS reporter undergoing a strategically important transition from on-premise PACS licensing to cloud-native SaaS delivery, with FY24/25 net sales of SEK 3,239.8M (+9.3% YoY) and underlying operating margin of 18.9%. The company satisfies all four eligibility criteria, offers three distinct ratio-analysis angles (profitability disruption, working-capital reshape, and currency translation), and aligns directly with my professional focus on Digital Health and medical imaging — providing both rigorous BUS 629 case material and applied market intelligence for my career trajectory.

---

## Background

**Company Overview.** Sectra AB (publ), founded in 1978 and headquartered in Linköping, Sweden, operates in three segments: Imaging IT Solutions (the dominant segment — PACS, RIS, and AI-enabled medical imaging), Secure Communications (encrypted defense and government systems), and Business Innovation (orthopedic surgical planning, oncology precision diagnostics). The United States is the largest market, followed by Sweden, the UK, and the Netherlands. KLAS Research has ranked Sectra #1 in PACS customer satisfaction for 13 consecutive years, with approximately 25% of new U.S. large-PACS purchase decisions.

**Selection Rationale.** Three converging reasons drove this choice. *First, industry alignment with my professional trajectory.* As a healthcare-technology professional with 23 years in Vietnam's medical equipment sector and Vice President of the Ho Chi Minh City Medical Equipment Association, I am studying the strategic direction of PACS and medical imaging IT — areas central to Vietnam's emerging Digital Health and AI agenda. Sectra, alongside one other major vendor, operates a cloud-native SaaS architecture that contrasts with the legacy on-premise PACS model dominating the industry for two decades. Selecting a company in this sector aligns my financial analysis training directly with my professional work — generating market intelligence on PACS and Digital Health, and informing how I will shape strategic business decisions in the medical technology sector going forward. *Second, analytical richness.* Sectra is undergoing a multi-year revenue model transition (on-premise license → cloud subscription) reshaping its profitability, working capital, and cash flow profiles simultaneously. The company also reports a SEK 110M non-recurring patent settlement in FY24/25, requiring analytical adjustment between reported and underlying metrics. *Third, international finance dimension.* Sectra reports in SEK while deriving the majority of revenue from non-SEK markets. FY24/25 marked the first year in recent memory where currency translation was no longer a tailwind.

## Method

**Data sources.** Primary data will come from Sectra's IFRS-audited Annual Reports for FY22/23, FY23/24, and FY24/25, supplemented by quarterly interim reports and the FY24/25 year-end press release — all available at investor.sectra.com. Market data (stock price, peer comparables including ASX-listed Pro Medicus as a pure-play SaaS PACS benchmark) sourced from Yahoo Finance and Stockanalysis.com; FX data from Riksbank.se. **Key considerations**: (i) Sectra's fiscal year runs May 1 – April 30, requiring care when benchmarking against calendar-year peers; (ii) IFRS reporting with SEK functional currency but USD/GBP/EUR transaction exposure; (iii) the SEK 110M patent settlement will be stripped from headline figures to compute underlying operating profit (SEK 613M underlying vs SEK 723M reported).

---

## Findings — Preliminary Hypotheses

**H1 (Working Capital).** I expect Sectra's Days Sales Outstanding to lengthen by 15–25 days FY23/24 → FY24/25 and deferred revenue to expand 25–40% **because** the SaaS transition shifts revenue-recognition timing: large multi-year contracts (the Québec 150-hospital cloud deal, multiple U.S. health-system 5-year SaaS agreements) generate upfront cash collections recognized as deferred revenue, while concurrent on-premise revenue declines tighten receivables turnover. Industry observation from emerging-market healthcare procurement (where OPEX-based contracting now dominates and public-hospital payment cycles extend to 12–24 months) suggests developed-market SaaS friction is directionally similar but less severe.

**H2 (Profitability).** I expect Sectra's underlying operating margin to compress 100–200 bps FY24/25 → FY25/26 — from 18.9% toward 17.0–18.0% — **because** the SaaS transition forces (a) decreasing high-margin on-premise license recognition and (b) increased upfront delivery costs for new SaaS customers who, per CEO Torbjörn Kronander (June 2025), "have not yet started to generate meaningful revenue." The CEO explicitly guided this margin pressure in the FY24/25 year-end commentary.

**H3 (Cash Conversion & FX).** I expect reported SEK revenue growth to lag underlying constant-currency growth by 100–150 bps in FY24/25 and cash conversion (CFO / Net Income) to remain above 1.5x **because** (a) ~50–60% of Sectra's revenue is in non-SEK currencies and prior-year FX tailwinds have reversed, and (b) SaaS prepayment dynamics structurally elevate operating cash flow above accrual earnings — already confirmed at 1.64x in FY24/25 (CFO SEK 922.4M / Net Income SEK 563.4M).

---

## Implications — Ratio Categories Preview

Given Sectra's ongoing on-premise-to-SaaS transition, the most analytically meaningful ratio categories are **profitability** (gross margin, operating margin — both reported and adjusted for the patent settlement; ROE, ROIC), **working capital efficiency** (DSO, deferred-revenue dynamics, cash conversion cycle), and **cash flow strength** (OCF / Net Income, FCF margin), where the SaaS model's hallmark cash-flow leadership emerges. Liquidity ratios warrant routine tracking. Leverage ratios are de-prioritized given Sectra's historically minimal long-term debt and AGM 2025's extraordinary dividend proposal — both signaling low capital-structure risk.
